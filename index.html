Jumbini

A dog who actually lives on your Mac.

Jumba walks along the tops of your open windows. He gets the zoomies when your Mac runs hot, and goes to sleep when you walk away from it. He is a real dog — a tricolor spaniel mix — and every frame of him was drawn by hand from photographs.

Three things that make him different

He walks on your windows.

Not the edge of the screen — your actual windows. He picks one, walks over, hops onto the title bar, patrols it, and leans over the edge to look down. Move the window and he rides it. Drag it hard enough and he falls off, which is the joke.

Most desktop pets pace along the bottom of the display. The ones that climb are Java, or Windows-only, or both.

He knows what your Mac is doing.

The machine gets hot and he gets the zoomies. Battery drops and he whines and lies down. Your build finishes and he celebrates. He never tells you any of this — no dashboard, no notification, no status bar.

There is just a dog behaving oddly, and a second later you work out why.

He is one real dog, and the art is not generated.

334 hand-drawn sprites across two coats — eight rotations for every state, drawn from photographs of an actual dog named Jumba. No catalog of ninety interchangeable pets. No marketplace. No "pick your character" screen.

There is one dog. That is the entire point.

He runs his own life

Every few seconds of standing around, he rolls the dice and picks something to do. Nothing here is scripted, and nothing is on a timer you can predict.

  • OddsWhat he does
  • 40%Wander somewhere new
  • 15%Nap — in his bed, if he has one
  • 12%Sniff your cursor around the screen
  • 10%Spin in place, for no reason
  • 8%Zoomies
  • 6%Hunch over and do his business
  • 5%Climb onto one of your windows
  • 4%Bark at nothing in particular

The sniffing one is the sleeper hit. He trots toward wherever your pointer is, and once he's close he drops his nose and keeps tracking it. Six times out of ten a finished sniff escalates — he goes into a stalk, freezes, and pounces on your cursor.

Five things he notices about your machine

Every reading is ambient. Nothing he watches needs a permission prompt, and a source that can't be read on your Mac switches itself off permanently rather than nagging you.

A build finishes Celebration — a yip, hearts, confetti. A rebuild storm still only gets one party.
The machine runs hot Zoomies. He is not helping.
Battery drops low He whines and lies down about it.
You step away Two minutes of no input and he curls up. You come back, he wakes up.
Focus turns on Best-effort, and usually off — Focus has no public API, and he will never ask you for Full Disk Access to get one.

Things to do with him

Fetch

Right-click → Fetch, then click anywhere. The ball arcs over, he sprints after it, carries it back to where he was standing, and does a little flourish about it.

Toys

A frisbee with real hang time, so the mid-air catch is possible. A squeaky toy he shakes to death. A tug rope that re-lays itself every frame — a genuine contest with a coin-flip ending.

Tricks and treats

Shake, High Five, Play Dead, Roll Over — and he knows none of them yet. Reward an attempt with a treat within ten seconds and it counts as a rep. Three reps and it's his, permanently.

Jumbini Cam

⌥⇧J copies him — whatever he happens to be doing right then — to your clipboard as a transparent PNG with a caption plate. Paste it into Slack. That's the whole feature.

What he doesn't do

Worth stating plainly, because a lot of things in this category do.

  • No network. He never phones home. There is nothing to phone home to.
  • No account, no login, no telemetry, no analytics.
  • No permissions. No Accessibility, no Screen Recording, no Full Disk Access, no Input Monitoring. He reads window geometry, idle time, battery and thermal state through public APIs that require no prompt, and reads nothing else.
  • No AI. No model, no API key, no chat. He is a state machine with 29 states and a dice roll, and he does not have anything to say to you.
  • No purchases, no cosmetic packs, no subscription, no ads.

What he is: 327 deterministic tests behind a pure state machine, and a build signed with a Developer ID certificate and notarized by Apple — which is how your Mac knows the app you downloaded is the one that left this repository.

Install

Requirements: macOS 14 (Sonoma) or newer, Apple Silicon.

  1. Download the DMG

    Grab Jumbini.dmg from the latest release.

    Jumbini.dmg

  2. Drag him to Applications

    Open the DMG and drag Jumbini across.

  3. Open it

    That is the whole thing. Jumbini is signed and notarized, so it opens on first launch like any other app — no right-click trick, no trip through Privacy & Security, no "Jumbini is damaged and can't be opened."

He has no Dock icon and no window. When he's running you get a dog on screen and a small pixel-dog icon in your menu bar — mute, pause, Jumbini Cam and Leave Jumbini Behind all live in there.

Or build him from source — no Xcode needed 
git clone https://github.com/abjumb/jumbini.git
cd jumbini
./Scripts/bundle.sh          # → build/Jumbini.app
open build/Jumbini.app

Command Line Tools and Swift 6 are enough. ./Scripts/test.sh runs the 327 tests; ./Scripts/dmg.sh builds the disk image.

Jumba is a real dog

The pixel art is of him, drawn by hand from photographs — not generated, not licensed, not bought from a sprite pack. Eight rotations for each of twenty states, twice over for his second haircut.

The code is MIT. The artwork isn't: Jumba stays his. Fork it, build it, reuse the code — and if you ship something derived from it, replace the dog.
